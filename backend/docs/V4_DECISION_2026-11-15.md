## What the two sides actually measure

| Input | PGDA 69 reference (source) | V4 tool / runtime | Comparable? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Visibility rule | Any part of the disc unobscured (product page; PDS label `AVGVISIB_85S_060M_201608.LBL`) | Body centre above the skyline (PRD runtime rule) | **No** |
| Averaging | Hourly time steps over 18.6 years (product page) | Daily at 00:00 UTC over 18.6 years from 2000-01-01 | Approximately; daily is noisier, not biased |
| Observer height | Surface; the page notes that height above the surface needs further analysis | 0 m (`validate_illumination.py`) | Yes |
| Terrain | LDEM_85S_060M (60 m), DE421 era | PGDA 90 20 m near + 160 m far | No; second-order |
| Ephemeris | DE421 era | SPICE DE440 | Negligible at this scale |

Source checked 15 Nov 2026: https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/69 ("averaged over hourly
timesteps spanning 18.6 years"; the Earth is "considered visible if any part of its disc is not
obscured"). The solar PDS label uses the same any-fraction rule (see `V4_SOURCE_REVIEW.md`).

The rule mismatch has the observed sign. A centre rule can never count more visibility than an
any-fraction rule, and the runtime is lower (7.8% < 14.1%). The recorded diagnostic
(`V4-disc-sensitivity.json`) adds a fixed 0.27° solar radius and closes 2.28 pp. That does not
pass, and a fixed radius is not a full disc model.
